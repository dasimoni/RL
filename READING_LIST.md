# Reading List

A curated, annotated guide to the books, courses and papers behind this course, and a route from its last chapter to the research frontier. It is built from the "Historical notes and key papers" and "Further reading" sections of every chapter. Each entry points to the chapter section it complements, so you can read the paper next to the derivation or experiment that depends on it.

**How this list is organised**

1. **Textbooks and monographs**: what each book is best for and which chapters it complements.
2. **Courses and lecture series**: video courses, lecture notes and practitioner resources.
3. **Papers by chapter**: 247 papers, in the order of Chapters 00–20. The 26 marked **★** are the "start here" essentials.
4. **A reading order to the research frontier**: what to read after the course, by research track.

**Conventions.** Papers are listed as **Title.** Authors. *Venue* year. Venues use their current names: NeurIPS was called NIPS before 2018. "arXiv" means the work circulated as a preprint, and a later venue is given where there is one. Author lists with more than five names are shortened to "et al.". Chapter links give the section number in the link text, for example [Ch 05 §8](chapters/05-temporal-difference.md) for the Q-learning section of Chapter 05. Symbols follow [NOTATION.md](NOTATION.md).

## 1. Textbooks and monographs

### 1.1 The core references

* **Sutton & Barto, *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*, 2nd ed. (MIT Press, 2018).** Free online. *Best for:* the conceptual core of the field and the notation of this course, which follows it. Short, readable chapters with exercises that complement ours. *Complements:* almost every chapter of Part I. S&B Ch. 2 ↔ [Ch 02](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md); Ch. 3 ↔ [Ch 01](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md); Ch. 4 ↔ [Ch 03](chapters/03-dynamic-programming.md); Ch. 5 ↔ [Ch 04](chapters/04-monte-carlo.md); Ch. 6 ↔ [Ch 05](chapters/05-temporal-difference.md); Ch. 7 and 12 ↔ [Ch 06](chapters/06-n-step-and-eligibility-traces.md); Ch. 8 ↔ [Ch 07](chapters/07-planning-and-learning-tabular.md); Ch. 9–11 ↔ [Ch 08](chapters/08-function-approximation.md); Ch. 13 ↔ [Ch 10](chapters/10-policy-gradients.md); Ch. 15 (neuroscience) ↔ [Ch 05 §14](chapters/05-temporal-difference.md); §16.5 (Atari DQN) ↔ [Ch 09](chapters/09-deep-q-learning.md); Ch. 17 ↔ [Ch 15](chapters/15-beyond-mdps.md).
* **Szepesvári, *Algorithms for Reinforcement Learning* (Morgan & Claypool, 2010).** Free PDF from the author. *Best for:* a short, mathematically tight tour of the operator view (contractions, fixed points), value prediction with function approximation, LSTD, gradient TD and early sample-complexity results. *Complements:* [Ch 01](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md), [Ch 03 §2](chapters/03-dynamic-programming.md), [Ch 08](chapters/08-function-approximation.md), [Ch 19](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
* **Agarwal, Jiang, Kakade & Sun, *Reinforcement Learning: Theory and Algorithms* (monograph, free online).** *Best for:* clean modern proofs: iteration complexity of VI and PI, the occupancy-measure LP, generative-model sample complexity, the simulation and performance difference lemmas, UCBVI, linear MDPs, Bellman rank, policy-gradient and natural-policy-gradient convergence, and offline RL. The best single companion to the theory chapter. *Complements:* [Ch 01](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md), [Ch 03](chapters/03-dynamic-programming.md), [Ch 11 §5](chapters/11-trust-regions-and-ppo.md), [Ch 13 §2](chapters/13-model-based-rl.md), [Ch 14 §3](chapters/14-exploration.md), [Ch 19](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
* **Murphy, *Reinforcement Learning: An Overview* (arXiv:2412.05265; first version December 2024, revised several times since).** *Best for:* a big-picture, up-to-date map of deep RL and sequential decision making: value-based, policy-based and model-based methods, multi-agent RL, RL and LLMs, offline RL, hierarchical RL and intrinsic reward, with pointers into the recent literature. Use it after the course to orient yourself before reading papers in a new area. *Complements:* Parts II and III ([Ch 09](chapters/09-deep-q-learning.md)–[Ch 18](chapters/18-rl-for-language-models.md)).
* **Kochenderfer, Wheeler & Wray, *Algorithms for Decision Making* (MIT Press, 2022).** Free online. *Best for:* a broad, code-oriented survey of sequential decision making under uncertainty, including beliefs, exact and approximate POMDP solvers and multi-agent settings. *Complements:* [Ch 01](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md), [Ch 15 §2](chapters/15-beyond-mdps.md), [Ch 17](chapters/17-multi-agent-rl.md).

### 1.2 Dynamic programming, optimal control and MDP theory

* **Puterman, *Markov Decision Processes: Discrete Stochastic Dynamic Programming* (Wiley, 1994).** *Best for:* the rigorous reference on MDPs: policy classes, existence of deterministic optimal policies under several criteria, VI, PI, modified PI, the LP formulation, action elimination and the average-reward criterion. Read its Chapter 6 for every theorem of our Chapter 03 in its most general form. *Complements:* [Ch 01 §6–13](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md), [Ch 03](chapters/03-dynamic-programming.md).
* **Bertsekas, *Dynamic Programming and Optimal Control*, Vols. I–II (Athena Scientific; Vol. I 4th ed. 2017, Vol. II 4th ed. 2012).** *Best for:* the control-theory perspective: finite-horizon problems, stochastic shortest paths (the right setting for undiscounted episodic tasks), asynchronous and distributed DP, and approximate DP with projected equations (Vol. II, Ch. 6). *Complements:* [Ch 01](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md), [Ch 03 §8, §11](chapters/03-dynamic-programming.md), [Ch 08 §5](chapters/08-function-approximation.md).
* **Bertsekas & Tsitsiklis, *Neuro-Dynamic Programming* (Athena Scientific, 1996).** *Best for:* the classical convergence theory of TD, Q-learning and approximate DP, written by the people who proved many of the results; contractions and asynchronous stochastic approximation. *Complements:* [Ch 00 §3–4](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md), [Ch 05 §5](chapters/05-temporal-difference.md), [Ch 08](chapters/08-function-approximation.md), [Ch 19 §3](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
* **Bertsekas, *Reinforcement Learning and Optimal Control* (Athena Scientific, 2019)** and ***Rollout, Policy Iteration, and Distributed Reinforcement Learning* (Athena Scientific, 2020).** *Best for:* rollout and decision-time planning seen as approximate policy iteration, from the optimal-control side. *Complements:* [Ch 07 §9–11](chapters/07-planning-and-learning-tabular.md), [Ch 13 §9](chapters/13-model-based-rl.md).
* **Altman, *Constrained Markov Decision Processes* (Chapman & Hall/CRC, 1999).** *Best for:* developing the occupancy-measure LP into the theory of constrained MDPs, the basis of much safe-RL work. *Complements:* [Ch 03 §10](chapters/03-dynamic-programming.md), [Ch 20 §11](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).

### 1.3 Bandits, online learning and experimentation

* **Lattimore & Szepesvári, *Bandit Algorithms* (Cambridge University Press, 2020).** Free version on the authors' website. *Best for:* the definitive modern reference, with complete proofs: concentration, UCB and its optimality, lower bounds (Ch. 13–16), adversarial bandits, linear and contextual bandits, pure exploration and Bayesian methods. *Complements:* [Ch 02](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md) (read its Ch. 7–8 after our §11.5, and Ch. 15–16 after §11.4–11.7), the bandit parts of [Ch 14](chapters/14-exploration.md), and the lower-bound techniques of [Ch 19 §7](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
* **Slivkins, *Introduction to Multi-Armed Bandits* (Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning, 2019).** *Best for:* a shorter, gentler textbook, with excellent chapters on lower bounds and on bandits with economic constraints. *Complements:* [Ch 02](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md).
* **Bubeck & Cesa-Bianchi, *Regret Analysis of Stochastic and Nonstochastic Multi-armed Bandit Problems* (Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning, 2012).** *Best for:* a compact survey of regret theory, including the clean EXP3 proof used in [Ch 02 §12](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md).
* **Russo, Van Roy, Kazerouni, Osband & Wen, *A Tutorial on Thompson Sampling* (Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning, 2018).** *Best for:* posterior sampling from Bernoulli bandits to RL, with practical advice on approximate posteriors and examples of when it beats optimism. *Complements:* [Ch 02 §7](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md), [Ch 14 §4](chapters/14-exploration.md).
* **Cesa-Bianchi & Lugosi, *Prediction, Learning, and Games* (Cambridge University Press, 2006).** *Best for:* online learning against adversaries, regret, Blackwell approachability and the link from regret to equilibria. *Complements:* [Ch 02 §12](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md), [Ch 17 §5](chapters/17-multi-agent-rl.md).
* **Gittins, Glazebrook & Weber, *Multi-armed Bandit Allocation Indices*, 2nd ed. (Wiley, 2011).** *Best for:* the Bayes-optimal side of bandits: Gittins indices, their proofs and extensions. *Complements:* [Ch 02](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md).
* **Kohavi, Tang & Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* (Cambridge University Press, 2020).** *Best for:* the practitioner's view of A/B testing, the necessary counterweight to "always use a bandit". *Complements:* [Ch 02 §14](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md).

### 1.4 Specialised monographs

* **Bellemare, Dabney & Rowland, *Distributional Reinforcement Learning* (MIT Press, 2023).** *Best for:* the definitive treatment of return distributions: distributional operators, probability metrics, projections and the control case. *Complements:* [Ch 09 §9](chapters/09-deep-q-learning.md).
* **Albrecht, Christianos & Schäfer, *Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Foundations and Modern Approaches* (MIT Press, 2024).** *Best for:* the textbook for multi-agent RL: game models, solution concepts, learning dynamics and deep MARL, with code. *Complements:* [Ch 17](chapters/17-multi-agent-rl.md); read it next after that chapter.
* **Lambert, *Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback* (online book at rlhfbook.com; also arXiv:2504.12501 and a Manning print edition, 2026).** *Best for:* the whole language-model post-training stack from a practitioner's angle: instruction tuning, reward modelling, regularisation, policy-gradient algorithms, direct alignment, RL with verifiable rewards, and over-optimisation. *Complements:* [Ch 18](chapters/18-rl-for-language-models.md), for the engineering detail the chapter skips.
* **Foster & Rakhlin, *Foundations of Reinforcement Learning and Interactive Decision Making* (lecture notes, arXiv:2312.16730, 2023).** *Best for:* a unified statistical treatment of bandits, contextual bandits and RL with function approximation through the decision–estimation coefficient. *Complements:* [Ch 02](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md), [Ch 14 §3–4](chapters/14-exploration.md), [Ch 19 §9](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
* **Shoham & Leyton-Brown, *Multiagent Systems: Algorithmic, Game-Theoretic, and Logical Foundations* (Cambridge University Press, 2009).** *Best for:* rigorous game theory for computer scientists, including extensive-form games, the sequence form and learning in games. *Complements:* [Ch 17 §3, §5, §10](chapters/17-multi-agent-rl.md).
* **Oliehoek & Amato, *A Concise Introduction to Decentralized POMDPs* (Springer, 2016).** *Best for:* the Dec-POMDP model, its complexity and planning algorithms, and the origin of the centralised-training, decentralised-execution viewpoint. *Complements:* [Ch 17 §2, §7](chapters/17-multi-agent-rl.md).
* **Nisan, Roughgarden, Tardos & Vazirani (eds.), *Algorithmic Game Theory* (Cambridge University Press, 2007).** *Best for:* the complexity of equilibria, the price of anarchy, and Blum and Mansour's chapter on learning, regret and equilibria. *Complements:* [Ch 17 §3, §5](chapters/17-multi-agent-rl.md).
* **Ghavamzadeh, Mannor, Pineau & Tamar, *Bayesian Reinforcement Learning: A Survey* (Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning, 2015).** *Best for:* the Bayes-adaptive view of exploration and meta-learning in depth. *Complements:* [Ch 14 §4](chapters/14-exploration.md), [Ch 15 §7](chapters/15-beyond-mdps.md).
* **Osa et al., *An Algorithmic Perspective on Imitation Learning* (Foundations and Trends in Robotics, 2018).** *Best for:* a broad survey of behaviour cloning, inverse RL and their use in robotics. *Complements:* [Ch 16 §2–5](chapters/16-offline-rl-and-imitation.md).
* **Ziebart, *Modeling Purposeful Adaptive Behavior with the Principle of Maximum Causal Entropy* (PhD thesis, Carnegie Mellon University, 2010).** *Best for:* the full maximum-entropy and maximum-causal-entropy story, with derivations. *Complements:* [Ch 12 §5](chapters/12-continuous-control-actor-critic.md), [Ch 16 §4](chapters/16-offline-rl-and-imitation.md).

### 1.5 Mathematical and machine-learning background

* **Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, *Deep Learning* (MIT Press, 2016).** Free online. Chapters 6–8 for MLPs, backpropagation and optimisation. *Complements:* [Ch 00 §7](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md).
* **Levin, Peres & Wilmer, *Markov Chains and Mixing Times*, 2nd ed. (AMS, 2017).** Chapters 1–4 cover stationary distributions, convergence and mixing at the right level of rigour. *Complements:* [Ch 00 §1](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md).
* **Owen, *Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples* (online book, 2013).** The best treatment of self-normalised importance sampling, effective sample size and the choice of proposals. *Complements:* [Ch 00 §2](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md), [Ch 04 §6–9](chapters/04-monte-carlo.md).
* **Cover & Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (Wiley, 2006).** Chapter 2 is the standard reference for entropy, KL divergence and Jensen's inequality. *Complements:* [Ch 00 §5](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md).
* **Borkar, *Stochastic Approximation: A Dynamical Systems Viewpoint* (Cambridge University Press, 2008)** and **Kushner & Yin, *Stochastic Approximation and Recursive Algorithms and Applications*, 2nd ed. (Springer, 2003).** The ODE method behind the convergence proofs of TD, Q-learning and two-timescale actor-critics, including the Borkar–Meyn stability theorem. *Complements:* [Ch 00 §3](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md), [Ch 19 §3](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
* **Nocedal & Wright, *Numerical Optimization*, 2nd ed. (Springer, 2006).** Chapter 4 (trust regions) and Chapter 5 (conjugate gradient). *Complements:* [Ch 11 §6](chapters/11-trust-regions-and-ppo.md).
* **Russell & Norvig, *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*, 4th ed. (Pearson, 2020).** The chapters on search and adversarial search: A\*, minimax, alpha–beta, the heuristic-search tradition behind RTDP and MCTS. *Complements:* [Ch 07 §9–12](chapters/07-planning-and-learning-tabular.md).

### 1.6 Historical classics

* **Bellman, *Dynamic Programming* (Princeton University Press, 1957).** The principle of optimality, the functional equation, successive approximation and the phrase "curse of dimensionality". *Complements:* [Ch 01](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md), [Ch 03](chapters/03-dynamic-programming.md).
* **Howard, *Dynamic Programming and Markov Processes* (MIT Press, 1960).** Policy iteration, from Howard's 1958 MIT thesis. *Complements:* [Ch 03 §5](chapters/03-dynamic-programming.md).

* **David Silver's UCL Course on Reinforcement Learning (2015).** Ten 90-minute lectures from the UCL graduate course, recorded and posted on YouTube, with slides on Silver's website. It covers MDPs, dynamic programming, model-free prediction and control, value function approximation, policy gradients, integrating learning and planning, and exploration. It is still the most widely watched introduction, close in spirit and notation to Sutton & Barto. *Pairs with:* [Ch 01](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md)–[Ch 10](chapters/10-policy-gradients.md) and [Ch 14](chapters/14-exploration.md).
* **DeepMind x UCL Reinforcement Learning Lecture Series (2021).** Thirteen lectures by Hado van Hasselt, Diana Borsa and Matteo Hessel, aimed at Master's level and above. It follows the structure of Sutton & Barto's second edition, from exploration and control, MDPs and DP, model-free prediction and control, function approximation, planning and models, policy gradients and actor-critics, to approximate DP, multi-step and off-policy learning, and deep RL. A more modern alternative to Silver's course. *Pairs with:* [Ch 01](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md)–[Ch 11](chapters/11-trust-regions-and-ppo.md).

The syllabus runs from imitation learning, policy gradients, actor-critics and value-based methods through advanced policy gradients, model-based RL, offline RL, exploration, control as inference and RL theory; recent offerings add RL for language models. Five programming assignments. The standard graduate deep-RL course. *Pairs with:* [Ch 09](chapters/09-deep-q-learning.md)–[Ch 16](chapters/16-offline-rl-and-imitation.md) and [Ch 18](chapters/18-rl-for-language-models.md).

## 3. Papers by chapter

Each chapter's list runs roughly through that chapter's sections, with closely related papers kept together and the classic before its follow-ups. Papers that matter to several chapters are listed once, where they are used most, with cross-references in the note.

### Ch 00 — [The Mathematical and ML Toolkit](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md)

- **A stochastic approximation method.** Robbins & Monro. *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 1951. The root-finding iteration and step-size conditions behind every incremental update in the course, from sample averages to TD; [Ch 00 §3](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md), [Ch 19 §3](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **Optimization methods for large-scale machine learning.** Bottou, Curtis & Nocedal. *SIAM Review* 2018. A rigorous but readable analysis of SGD, including the noise floor of constant step sizes; [Ch 00 §3](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md).
- **Adam: A method for stochastic optimization.** Kingma & Ba. *ICLR* 2015. The adaptive optimiser used by default in most deep-RL code; know what its moment estimates do before tuning it; [Ch 00 §3, §7](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md).
- **Likelihood ratio gradient estimation for stochastic systems.** Glynn. *Communications of the ACM* 1990. The score-function estimator from simulation optimisation, before RL called it REINFORCE; [Ch 00 §6](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md).
- **Auto-encoding variational Bayes.** Kingma & Welling. *ICLR* 2014. Made reparameterisation (pathwise) gradients mainstream, the estimator SAC's actor update relies on; [Ch 00 §6](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md), [Ch 12 §6](chapters/12-continuous-control-actor-critic.md).
- **Monte Carlo gradient estimation in machine learning.** Mohamed, Rosca, Figurnov & Mnih. *JMLR* 2020. The definitive survey of score-function, pathwise and measure-valued estimators, with variance analyses; [Ch 00 §6](chapters/00-math-toolkit.md).

### Ch 01 — [The RL Problem and MDPs](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md)

- **Some studies in machine learning using the game of checkers.** Samuel. *IBM Journal of Research and Development* 1959. A checkers player that moved its evaluation of a position toward the evaluation of later positions: an early form of temporal-difference learning, and still a pleasure to read; [Ch 01](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md) historical notes.
- **A Markovian decision process.** Bellman. *Journal of Mathematics and Mechanics* 1957. The formulation of Markov decision processes; [Ch 01 §6](chapters/01-the-rl-problem.md).

- **An empirical evaluation of Thompson sampling.** Chapelle & Li. *NeurIPS* 2011. The paper that revived Thompson sampling by showing its practical strength, including on display-advertising and news-recommendation data; [Ch 02 §7](chapters/02-multi-armed-bandits.md).

- **Model-based reinforcement learning with a generative model is minimax optimal.** Agarwal, Kakade & Yang. *COLT* 2020. Plug-in planning on the empirical model achieves the minimax rate for policies; [Ch 19 §5](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **Finite-time bounds for fitted value iteration.** Munos & Szepesvári. *JMLR* 2008. Weighted-norm error propagation and concentrability coefficients for approximate DP; [Ch 19 §5, §10](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **On the sample complexity of reinforcement learning.** Kakade. *PhD thesis, University College London* 2003. Defines the sample complexity of exploration and refines the PAC-MDP analysis; [Ch 19 §6](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **Is Q-learning provably efficient?** Jin, Allen-Zhu, Bubeck & Jordan. *NeurIPS* 2018. A short, readable regret proof for model-free Q-learning with UCB bonuses and the learning-rate lemma; [Ch 19 §7](chapters/19-rl-theory.md), [Ch 14 §3](chapters/14-exploration.md).
- ★ **On the theory of policy gradient methods: Optimality, approximation, and distribution shift.** Agarwal, Kakade, Lee & Mahajan. *JMLR* 2021 (COLT 2020). Gradient domination, global convergence of softmax and log-barrier PG, the dimension-free NPG rate, and NPG with function approximation; [Ch 19 §8](chapters/19-rl-theory.md), [Ch 11 §5](chapters/11-trust-regions-and-ppo.md).
- **On the global convergence rates of softmax policy gradient methods.** Mei, Xiao, Szepesvári & Schuurmans. *ICML* 2020. The $O(1/t)$ rate for exact softmax PG and its plateaus; [Ch 19 §8](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **Contextual decision processes with low Bellman rank are PAC-learnable.** Jiang, Krishnamurthy, Agarwal, Langford & Schapire. *ICML* 2017. Bellman rank and the OLIVE algorithm, an early structural condition for sample-efficient RL with rich observations under realizability alone; [Ch 19 §9](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **Provably efficient reinforcement learning with linear function approximation.** Jin, Yang, Wang & Jordan. *COLT* 2020. Linear MDPs and LSVI-UCB, optimistic least-squares value iteration with $\tilde O(\sqrt{d^3H^3T})$ regret independent of the numbers of states and actions; [Ch 19 §9](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **Exponential lower bounds for planning in MDPs with linearly-realizable optimal action-value functions.** Weisz, Amortila & Szepesvári. *ALT* 2021. Even with a generative model and an exactly linear $q_\ast$, a planner can need a number of queries exponential in the feature dimension or the horizon; why horizon makes function approximation hard; [Ch 19 §9](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **The statistical complexity of interactive decision making.** Foster, Kakade, Qian & Rakhlin. *arXiv* 2021. The decision–estimation coefficient, a unifying measure of the difficulty of interactive learning; [Ch 19 §9](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **Is pessimism provably efficient for offline RL?** Jin, Yang & Wang. *ICML* 2021. Pessimistic value iteration and its guarantees under partial coverage; [Ch 19 §10](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).
- **Bridging offline reinforcement learning and imitation learning: A tale of pessimism.** Rashidinejad, Zhu, Ma, Jiao & Russell. *NeurIPS* 2021. Single-policy concentrability and pessimism interpolating between imitation and offline RL; [Ch 19 §10](chapters/19-rl-theory.md).

### Ch 20 — [Deep RL in Practice](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md)

- ★ **Policy invariance under reward transformations: Theory and application to reward shaping.** Ng, Harada & Russell. *ICML* 1999. Potential-based shaping is the only shaping that provably preserves optimal policies; a short paper whose proof is worth reading; [Ch 20 §2](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md), [Ch 16 §3](chapters/16-offline-rl-and-imitation.md).
- **Potential-based shaping and Q-value initialization are equivalent.** Wiewiora. *JAIR* 2003. Explains why shaping and initialisation give identical learning; [Ch 20 §2](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Concrete problems in AI safety.** Amodei et al. *arXiv* 2016. Names reward hacking, safe exploration and distributional shift as research problems; [Ch 20 §2, §11](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Goal misgeneralization in deep reinforcement learning.** Langosco, Koch, Sharkey, Pfau & Krueger. *ICML* 2022. Agents that keep their capabilities but pursue the wrong goal out of distribution; [Ch 20 §2](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- ★ **Deep reinforcement learning that matters.** Henderson et al. *AAAI* 2018. Seeds, hyperparameters and codebases change conclusions; the paper that made the field take reproducibility seriously; read the figures; [Ch 20 §5](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Revisiting the Arcade Learning Environment: Evaluation protocols and open problems for general agents.** Machado et al. *JAIR* 2018. Sticky actions and honest Atari evaluation protocols; [Ch 20 §6](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md), [Ch 09 §13](chapters/09-deep-q-learning.md).
- ★ **Deep reinforcement learning at the edge of the statistical precipice.** Agarwal, Schwarzer, Castro, Courville & Bellemare. *NeurIPS* 2021 (Outstanding Paper Award). IQM, stratified-bootstrap confidence intervals and performance profiles, with the `rliable` library; [Ch 20 §6](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Empirical design in reinforcement learning.** Patterson, Neumann, White & White. *JMLR* 2024. A long, practical guide to experiments that support their conclusions, including hyperparameter selection; [Ch 20 §5–6](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **CleanRL: High-quality single-file implementations of deep reinforcement learning algorithms.** Huang et al. *JMLR* 2022. Single-file, benchmarked implementations close in spirit to this course's code; [Ch 20 §10](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **A comprehensive survey on safe reinforcement learning.** García & Fernández. *JMLR* 2015. The map of safe-RL approaches; [Ch 20 §11](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Constrained policy optimization.** Achiam, Held, Tamar & Abbeel. *ICML* 2017. Trust-region policy search for constrained MDPs, and the average-divergence bound used in [Ch 11 §3](chapters/11-trust-regions-and-ppo.md); [Ch 20 §11](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Domain randomization for transferring deep neural networks from simulation to the real world.** Tobin et al. *IROS* 2017. Visual randomisation as the basis of sim-to-real transfer; [Ch 20 §12](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Learning agile and dynamic motor skills for legged robots.** Hwangbo et al. *Science Robotics* 2019. Simulation-trained policies transferred to the ANYmal quadruped with a learned actuator model; [Ch 20 §13](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Autonomous navigation of stratospheric balloons using reinforcement learning.** Bellemare et al. *Nature* 2020. Distributional deep RL (QR-DQN) for Loon station-keeping, validated in a 39-day experiment over the Pacific; [Ch 20 §13](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Magnetic control of tokamak plasmas through deep reinforcement learning.** Degrave et al. *Nature* 2022. A controller trained in simulation and deployed zero-shot on the TCV tokamak, with an asymmetric actor-critic; [Ch 20 §13](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).
- **Challenges of real-world reinforcement learning: Definitions, benchmarks and analysis.** Dulac-Arnold et al. *Machine Learning* 2021. What separates benchmarks from deployments, with perturbations to test against; [Ch 20 §13](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md).

## 4. A reading order to the research frontier

This route assumes you have finished the course (or the full 01 → 20 path in the [README](README.md)). It has five stages. Stages 1–3 take everyone to the same place; in Stage 4 you choose one or two tracks. Throughout, keep the habits of [Ch 20 §14](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md): re-implement, read code, keep a lab notebook, use proper statistics, and watch the agent.

### Stage 1 — The essentials, in order

Read the 26 ★ papers in this order. For each deep-RL paper, reproduce one figure or table with your own code before moving on.

1. Foundations: Auer, Cesa-Bianchi & Fischer (2002) → Sutton (1988) → Tsitsiklis & Van Roy (1997).
2. Value-based deep RL: Mnih et al. (2015).
3. Policy optimisation: Williams (1992) → Sutton, McAllester, Singh & Mansour (2000) → Schulman et al. GAE (2016) → TRPO (2015) → PPO (2017).
4. Continuous control: TD3 (2018) → SAC (2018).
5. Planning and models: AlphaZero (2018) → DreamerV3 (2025).
6. Beyond the basic setting: Osband et al. (2019) → Sutton, Precup & Singh (1999) → DAgger (2011) → Levine et al. (2020) → CFR (2007).
7. Language models: Christiano et al. (2017) → InstructGPT (2022) → DPO (2023) → DeepSeek-R1 (2025).
8. Theory and practice: Agarwal, Kakade, Lee & Mahajan (2021) → Ng, Harada & Russell (1999) → Henderson et al. (2018) → Agarwal et al. (2021).

### Stage 2 — How the field checks its claims

Before trusting any comparison, including your own, read the empirical-methodology papers in this order: Henderson et al. (2018) → Machado et al. (2018) → Agarwal et al. (2021) → Patterson et al. (2024) → Engstrom et al. (2020) → Andrychowicz et al. (2021) → Huang et al., "The 37 implementation details of PPO" (2022). If you are heading for language models, add Huang et al., "The N+ implementation details of RLHF with PPO" (2024). After this stage, apply to every applied paper the four questions that [Ch 20](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md)'s "Further reading" asks of the landmark applications of its §13: what was the reward, what was the simulator, what were the baselines, and how many runs?

### Stage 3 — One book, read properly

Pick the monograph that matches your direction (§1) and work through it with the exercises: Agarwal–Jiang–Kakade–Sun for theory; Lattimore & Szepesvári for bandits and exploration; Bellemare–Dabney–Rowland for distributional RL; Albrecht–Christianos–Schäfer for multi-agent RL; Lambert for language-model post-training. Keep Murphy's *Reinforcement Learning: An Overview* at hand as a map of where each subfield's recent literature lives.

### Stage 5 — Staying current

* **Proceedings.** Follow NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, the Reinforcement Learning Conference (RLC) and CoRL, as [Ch 20 §14](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md) suggests; for theory add COLT and ALT. Read the orals and the papers that re-evaluate earlier claims, not only the new state of the art.
* **Re-read the overviews.** Murphy's overview and Lambert's RLHF book are revised as the field moves; the course chapters' "Further reading" lists tell you which classics the new work builds on.
* **Reproduce before you extend.** Pick a recent paper in your track, reproduce its main figure with ten seeds and the statistics of [Ch 20 §6](chapters/20-deep-rl-in-practice.md), and only then try to improve on it. Most research ideas come from the discrepancies you find.
